Topic 1

Differentiability & First Principles

The derivative at a point is the instantaneous rate of change — the slope of the tangent line. It is defined as a limit of slopes of secant lines as the two points collapse together.

Definition — Derivative (First Principles)
f'(x) = lim(h→0) [f(x+h) − f(x)] / h
f is differentiable at x if this limit exists and is finite. Equivalent form at a specific point a:
f'(a) = lim(x→a) [f(x)−f(a)]/(x−a)

Geometrically: the derivative is the slope of the unique tangent line to the graph of f at (a, f(a)).
Theorem — Differentiability Implies Continuity
Differentiable ⟹ Continuous (converse FALSE)
If f is differentiable at a, then f is continuous at a.
Proof: lim(x→a)[f(x)−f(a)] = lim(x→a) [f(x)−f(a)]/(x−a) · (x−a) = f'(a)·0 = 0. So lim f(x)=f(a). □

Counterexample to converse: f(x)=|x| is continuous at 0 but f'(0) does not exist (left slope = −1 ≠ +1 = right slope).

Proof — d/dx[x²] = 2xFirst Principles
f'(x) = lim(h→0) [(x+h)² − x²]/h = lim(h→0) [x²+2xh+h²−x²]/h = lim(h→0)[2x+h] = 2x. □
Proof — d/dx[sin x] = cos xFirst Principles + Fundamental Limits
f'(x) = lim(h→0)[sin(x+h)−sin(x)]/h
= lim(h→0)[sin(x)cos(h)+cos(x)sin(h)−sin(x)]/h
= sin(x)·lim(h→0)(cos(h)−1)/h + cos(x)·lim(h→0)sin(h)/h
= sin(x)·0 + cos(x)·1 = cos(x). □
Topic 2

The Four Differentiation Rules

Power Rule
d/dx[xⁿ] = n·xⁿ⁻¹ for any n ∈ ℝ d/dx[x⁵] = 5x⁴, d/dx[x^(1/2)] = (1/2)x^(-1/2) = 1/(2√x)
Theorem — Product Rule
(fg)' = f'g + fg'
If f and g are differentiable: (fg)' = f'g + fg'. Mnemonic: "derivative of first times second, plus first times derivative of second."
Proof — Product RuleFirst Principles (add-and-subtract trick)
[f(x+h)g(x+h) − f(x)g(x)]/h
Add and subtract f(x+h)g(x):
= [f(x+h)g(x+h) − f(x+h)g(x) + f(x+h)g(x) − f(x)g(x)]/h
= f(x+h)·[g(x+h)−g(x)]/h + g(x)·[f(x+h)−f(x)]/h
→ f(x)·g'(x) + g(x)·f'(x)  as h→0 (using f continuity: f(x+h)→f(x)). □
Theorem — Quotient Rule
(f/g)' = (f'g − fg') / g²
Mnemonic: "low d-high minus high d-low, over low squared." Requires g ≠ 0.
Theorem — Chain Rule
d/dx[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) · g'(x)
The most powerful rule. Applied outside-in: differentiate the outer function evaluated at the inner function, then multiply by the derivative of the inner function. In Leibniz: dy/dx = (dy/du)·(du/dx).

Common error: forgetting to multiply by the inner derivative. Every composition demands it.
Worked Example — Chain Rule (multiple layers)
Differentiate h(x) = e^(sin(x²)).
Outermost layer: eᵘ at u=sin(x²). Derivative: eᵘ = e^(sin(x²)).
Middle layer: sin(v) at v=x². Derivative: cos(v) = cos(x²).
Innermost: x². Derivative: 2x.
h'(x) = e^(sin(x²)) · cos(x²) · 2x.
Topic 3

Derivatives of Special Functions

Complete Derivative Reference Table
d/dx[eˣ] = eˣ d/dx[aˣ] = aˣ ln(a) d/dx[ln|x|] = 1/x d/dx[logₐ|x|] = 1/(x ln a) d/dx[sin x] = cos x d/dx[cos x] = −sin x d/dx[tan x] = sec²x d/dx[sec x] = sec x tan x d/dx[csc x] = −csc x cot x d/dx[cot x] = −csc²x d/dx[arcsin x] = 1/√(1−x²) d/dx[arccos x] = −1/√(1−x²) d/dx[arctan x] = 1/(1+x²)
Proof — d/dx[ln x] = 1/xFirst Principles + fundamental log limit
d/dx[ln x] = lim(h→0)[ln(x+h)−ln(x)]/h = lim(h→0)(1/h)ln((x+h)/x)
= lim(h→0)(1/x)·(x/h)·ln(1+h/x).
Let u=h/x → 0 as h→0:
= (1/x)·lim(u→0) ln(1+u)/u = (1/x)·1 = 1/x. □
Worked Example — Logarithmic Differentiation
Differentiate f(x) = xˣ (not a power rule case — the exponent is variable).
Take ln: ln f(x) = x·ln(x).
Differentiate both sides: f'(x)/f(x) = ln(x) + x·(1/x) = ln(x) + 1.
Multiply by f(x): f'(x) = xˣ·(ln(x)+1).

Practice Problems

01From first principles, prove d/dx[x³] = 3x².Easy▾
lim(h→0)[(x+h)³−x³]/h = lim[3x²h+3xh²+h³]/h = lim[3x²+3xh+h²] = 3x². □
02Differentiate f(x) = (x²+1)·ln(x).Easy▾
Product rule: f'(x) = 2x·ln(x) + (x²+1)·(1/x) = 2x·ln(x) + x + 1/x.
03Differentiate g(x) = ln(x)/sin(x) using Quotient Rule.Medium▾
g'(x)=[(1/x)sin(x)−ln(x)cos(x)]/sin²(x) = [sin(x)/x − ln(x)cos(x)]/sin²(x).
04Differentiate h(x) = arctan(e^(3x)).Hard▾
Chain rule (2 layers). Outer: arctan(u), derivative 1/(1+u²) at u=e^(3x): gives 1/(1+e^(6x)). Inner: e^(3x), derivative 3e^(3x). So h'(x) = 3e^(3x)/(1+e^(6x)).

Question 1
If f is differentiable at x=a, which must be true?
Recall the theorem about differentiability and continuity — and its converse.
Af is differentiable in a neighbourhood of a.
Bf is continuous at a.
Cf' is continuous at a.
Df is twice differentiable at a.
Correct! B. Differentiability at a point implies continuity at that point (theorem proved in this unit). A is false — differentiability at one point says nothing about nearby points. C is false — f could be differentiable at a but f' not continuous there (example: f(x)=x²sin(1/x), f(0)=0). D is false — a function can be once but not twice differentiable.
Question 2
What is d/dx[x^x]?
This is NOT a power rule case. The exponent is variable — you need logarithmic differentiation.
Ax · x^(x−1)
Bx^x · ln(x)
Cx^x · (ln(x) + 1)
Dx · x^(x−1) · ln(x)
Correct! C. Power rule only applies when the exponent is a constant. For xˣ: take ln y = x·ln x, differentiate to get y'/y = ln x + 1, so y' = xˣ(ln x + 1). Option A wrongly applies power rule. Option B forgets the +1 from the product rule on x·ln x.
Question 3
In the first-principles definition f'(a) = lim(h→0)[f(a+h)−f(a)]/h, the expression [f(a+h)−f(a)]/h represents:
AThe slope of the tangent line to f at a.
BThe slope of the secant line through (a, f(a)) and (a+h, f(a+h)).
CThe instantaneous rate of change of f at a.
DThe average of f over [a, a+h].
Correct! B. Before taking the limit, [f(a+h)−f(a)]/h is the slope of the secant line connecting two points on the graph. Taking the limit as h→0 collapses this to the tangent slope. A and C are what f'(a) equals AFTER taking the limit — not what the expression equals before. D is unrelated.

Differentiate f(x) = x³·eˣ. Write your answer in fully simplified form.

Question 5
Find the equation of the tangent line to f(x) = ln(x) at x = e. Write in y = mx + b form.
y = (1/e)x. f(e)=ln(e)=1. f'(x)=1/x, f'(e)=1/e. Tangent: y−1=(1/e)(x−e) ⟹ y=(1/e)x−1+1=(1/e)x.

Question 6 — First Principles Proof
Prove from first principles that d/dx[eˣ] = eˣ, using the known limit lim(h→0)(eʰ−1)/h = 1. Show every algebraic step.
Model Solution
d/dx[eˣ] = lim(h→0)[e^(x+h) − eˣ]/h
= lim(h→0)[eˣ·eʰ − eˣ]/h
= lim(h→0) eˣ·(eʰ−1)/h
= eˣ · lim(h→0)(eʰ−1)/h
= eˣ · 1 = eˣ. □

(The key step is factoring out eˣ — constant with respect to h — so it passes through the limit.)
Question 7 — Chain + Product
Differentiate f(x) = x²·sin(ln x). Show which rules you apply at each step.
Model Solution
Structure: Product of u(x)=x² and v(x)=sin(ln x).

u'(x): d/dx[x²] = 2x.

v'(x): d/dx[sin(ln x)] = cos(ln x) · d/dx[ln x] = cos(ln x) · (1/x). (Chain Rule)

Product Rule: f'(x) = u'v + uv' = 2x·sin(ln x) + x²·cos(ln x)/x
= 2x·sin(ln x) + x·cos(ln x)
= x[2sin(ln x) + cos(ln x)].
