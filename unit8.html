Topic 1

Local & Global Extrema

Definitions
Local and Global Extrema
Local max at c: f(c)≥f(x) for all x in some open interval containing c.
Local min at c: f(c)≤f(x) for all x in some open interval containing c.

Global (absolute) max on D: f(c)≥f(x) for all x∈D.
Every global extremum is local, but not vice versa.
Theorem — Fermat's Theorem
Local extrema at differentiable points require f'(c) = 0
If f has a local extremum at c AND f is differentiable at c, then f'(c)=0. This is necessary but not sufficient: f'(c)=0 doesn't guarantee an extremum (e.g., f(x)=x³ at c=0).
Definition — Critical Point
f'(c) = 0 OR f'(c) does not exist
Critical points are the candidates for local extrema. Every local extremum is a critical point (Fermat), but not every critical point is an extremum.
Topic 2

First & Second Derivative Tests

Theorem — First Derivative Test
Sign changes of f' classify local extrema
Let c be a critical point.
• f' changes + → − at c: local max at c.
• f' changes − → + at c: local min at c.
• f' doesn't change sign: neither (e.g. inflection point).
Theorem — Second Derivative Test
Concavity at a critical point classifies it
If f'(c)=0:
• f''(c)>0 ⟹ local min at c (concave up = "smile").
• f''(c)<0 ⟹ local max at c (concave down = "frown").
• f''(c)=0 ⟹ inconclusive; use 1st Derivative Test.
Concavity & Inflection Points
f''(x) > 0 on (a,b) ⟹ f is concave UP on (a,b) (slopes increasing) f''(x) < 0 on (a,b) ⟹ f is concave DOWN on (a,b) (slopes decreasing) Inflection point at c: f''(c)=0 (or undefined) AND f'' changes sign at c

Topic 3

Complete Curve Sketching

Curve Sketching Algorithm (8 steps)
1. Domain: where is f defined? 2. Intercepts: f(0); solutions to f(x)=0. 3. Symmetry: even (f(−x)=f(x)), odd (f(−x)=−f(x)), neither. 4. Asymptotes: vertical (discontinuities), horizontal (limits at ±∞). 5. f'(x): find critical points, determine intervals of increase/decrease. 6. f''(x): find inflection points, determine intervals of concavity. 7. Classify each critical point via 1st or 2nd Derivative Test. 8. Plot key points and sketch.
Full Worked Example
Sketch f(x) = x⁴ − 4x³.
Domain: ℝ. y-intercept: f(0)=0. x-intercepts: x³(x−4)=0 ⟹ x=0,4.
f'(x)=4x³−12x²=4x²(x−3)=0 ⟹ critical points x=0,3.
Sign of f': x∈(−∞,0): 4x²>0,(x−3)<0, f'<0 (dec). (0,3): same signs, f'<0 (dec). (3,∞): f'>0 (inc).
Classification: x=0: no sign change in f' → inflection. x=3: f' changes −→+ → local min. f(3)=81−108=−27.
f''(x)=12x²−24x=12x(x−2)=0 ⟹ potential inflections x=0,2. Sign changes at both → inflection points at (0,0) and (2,−16).
Behavior: f(x)→+∞ as x→±∞. Global min at (3,−27).

Question 1
f'(c) = 0. Can we conclude f has a local extremum at c?
Think carefully: is f'(c)=0 sufficient, or just necessary?
AYes — by Fermat's Theorem, f'(c)=0 guarantees a local extremum.
BNo — f'(c)=0 is necessary but not sufficient. Example: f(x)=x³ at c=0.
CYes — unless f''(c)=0 as well.
DOnly if f is defined on a closed interval.
Correct! B. Fermat's Theorem says local extremum → f'(c)=0 (at differentiable points). The converse is false. f(x)=x³ has f'(0)=0 but a sign chart shows f' doesn't change sign at 0 — it's an inflection point, not an extremum.
Question 2
f''(c) = 0 at a critical point c. What can you conclude?
Ac is an inflection point.
Bc is neither a max nor a min.
CThe Second Derivative Test is inconclusive; use the First Derivative Test instead.
Dc is a local minimum since f'' is zero (flat = stable).
Correct! C. f''(c)=0 makes the 2nd Derivative Test inconclusive — all three outcomes (local max, local min, inflection) are possible. f(x)=x⁴ has f''(0)=0 at its global minimum; f(x)=x³ has f''(0)=0 at an inflection point; f(x)=−x⁴ has f''(0)=0 at its global maximum. You must check f' sign change.
Question 3
An inflection point of f occurs where:
Af''(c) = 0.
Bf'(c) = 0 and f''(c) = 0.
Cf''(c) = 0 (or undefined) AND f'' changes sign at c.
Df'(c) = 0 and the graph changes from concave up to concave down.
Correct! C. f''(c)=0 alone is insufficient (e.g. f(x)=x⁴ has f''(0)=0 but no inflection at 0 since f'' doesn't change sign). D is wrong because inflection points don't require f'(c)=0 (the slope at an inflection can be any value).

Question 7 — Proof via Derivatives
Prove that f(x) = x³ has no local extremum at x = 0, even though f'(0) = 0. Use the First Derivative Test, showing explicitly that f' does not change sign.
Model Solution
Compute f': f'(x) = 3x².

Sign analysis of f':
For x < 0 (near 0): f'(x) = 3x² > 0. (f is increasing to the left of 0.)
For x > 0 (near 0): f'(x) = 3x² > 0. (f is increasing to the right of 0.)

Conclusion by First Derivative Test: f' does not change sign at x=0 — it is positive on both sides. Therefore f is increasing through 0, and x=0 is NOT a local extremum.

It is an inflection point (f goes from concave down to concave up: f''(x)=6x changes sign at 0). □
