Topic 1

Implicit Differentiation

When a curve is defined by F(x,y)=0 rather than y=f(x), differentiate both sides with respect to x, treating y as a function of x. Every term involving y acquires a dy/dx factor via the Chain Rule. Then solve for dy/dx algebraically.

Method — Implicit Differentiation Step by Step
Given F(x,y) = 0: 1. Differentiate both sides w.r.t. x. 2. For each y-term: d/dx[g(y)] = g'(y)·(dy/dx) [Chain Rule] 3. Collect all dy/dx terms on one side. 4. Factor and solve: dy/dx = [remaining terms] / [coefficient of dy/dx]
Worked Example — Folium of Descartes
Find dy/dx for x³ + y³ = 6xy.
Differentiate: 3x² + 3y²(dy/dx) = 6y + 6x(dy/dx).
Collect dy/dx: 3y²(dy/dx) − 6x(dy/dx) = 6y − 3x².
(dy/dx)(3y² − 6x) = 6y − 3x².
dy/dx = (6y − 3x²)/(3y² − 6x) = (2y − x²)/(y² − 2x).

Topic 2

Extreme Value Theorem

Theorem — Extreme Value Theorem (EVT)
Continuous f on [a,b] attains its maximum and minimum
If f: [a,b]→ℝ is continuous, then f attains an absolute maximum value f(c) and an absolute minimum value f(d) for some c, d ∈ [a,b].

Both conditions — closed and continuous — are necessary. On an open interval (a,b), f may not attain its supremum. A discontinuous function on [a,b] may not either.
Closed Interval Method — Finding Absolute Extrema
1. Find all critical points in (a,b): solve f'(x)=0 and find where f'(x) is undefined. 2. Evaluate f at each critical point in (a,b). 3. Evaluate f at the endpoints: f(a) and f(b). 4. Largest value = absolute maximum. Smallest = absolute minimum.
Worked Example
Find absolute extrema of f(x) = x³ − 3x on [−2, 3].
f'(x)=3x²−3=3(x²−1)=0 ⟹ x=±1. Both in [−2,3]. ✓
Values: f(−2)=−8+6=−2. f(−1)=−1+3=2. f(1)=1−3=−2. f(3)=27−9=18.
Absolute max: f(3)=18. Absolute min: f(1)=f(−2)=−2 (tie).
Topic 3

Related Rates

Two quantities are related by an equation; both change with time. Differentiating the equation implicitly with respect to t connects their rates. The challenge is always: draw a picture, write the relationship, differentiate.

Related Rates — Problem-Solving Template
1. Draw a diagram. Label all time-dependent quantities as functions of t. 2. Write a geometric/physical equation relating the quantities. 3. Differentiate both sides w.r.t. t (implicit differentiation). 4. Substitute the known values and known rates at the instant asked. 5. Solve for the unknown rate.
Classic Example — Ladder Problem
A 10m ladder leans against a wall. Its base slides away at 2 m/s. How fast is the top sliding down when the base is 6m from the wall?
Let x = distance of base from wall, y = height of top. x² + y² = 100.
Differentiate w.r.t. t: 2x(dx/dt) + 2y(dy/dt) = 0.
When x=6: y=√(100−36)=8. Given dx/dt=2.
2(6)(2) + 2(8)(dy/dt)=0 ⟹ 24+16(dy/dt)=0 ⟹ dy/dt = −3/2 m/s. (Negative = downward.)

02Find absolute extrema of f(x)=2x³−9x²+12x on [0,3].Medium▾
f'(x)=6x²−18x+12=6(x−1)(x−2)=0 ⟹ x=1,2. f(0)=0, f(1)=5, f(2)=4, f(3)=9. Abs max=9 at x=3. Abs min=0 at x=0.
03A spherical balloon inflates at 10 cm³/s. How fast is the radius growing when r=5 cm?Hard▾
V=(4/3)πr³. dV/dt=4πr²(dr/dt). At r=5: 10=4π(25)(dr/dt). dr/dt=10/(100π)=1/(10π) cm/s.

A2y

Question 2
EVT guarantees that f attains its maximum on [a,b]. If f is continuous on (a,b) (open interval) only, then:
Af still attains a maximum — continuity is sufficient.
Bf may not attain a maximum; e.g., f(x)=x on (0,1) has no maximum value.
Cf attains a maximum only if it is differentiable.
DThe EVT still applies; the closed/open distinction doesn't matter.
Correct! B. The EVT requires a closed interval. On (0,1), f(x)=x is continuous and bounded above by 1, but never equals 1 on the open interval — no maximum is attained. The supremum is 1 but it's not achieved.
Question 3
In a related rates problem, two quantities x(t) and y(t) satisfy x² + y² = 25. At a given moment, x=3, y=4, and dx/dt = −1. What is dy/dt?
A−4/3
B3/4
C−3/4
D4/3
Correct! B. Differentiate: 2x(dx/dt)+2y(dy/dt)=0. Substitute x=3, y=4, dx/dt=−1: 2(3)(−1)+2(4)(dy/dt)=0 ⟹ −6+8(dy/dt)=0 ⟹ dy/dt=6/8=3/4. Positive: y is increasing (the point moves so that as x decreases, y increases — moving along the circle).

Find dy/dx for sin(xy) = x. Leave your answer in terms of x and y.
dy/dx = (1 − y·cos(xy))/(x·cos(xy)). Differentiate: cos(xy)·(y + x·dy/dx) = 1. Expand: y·cos(xy) + x·cos(xy)·dy/dx = 1. Solve: dy/dx = (1−y·cos(xy))/(x·cos(xy)).
Question 5
At what value of x does f(x) = x − 2√x achieve its minimum on [0, 9]? (Give x as a number.)
x = 1. f'(x)=1−1/√x=0 ⟹ √x=1 ⟹ x=1. Values: f(0)=0, f(1)=1−2=−1, f(9)=9−6=3. Minimum is −1 at x=1.

Question 6 — Implicit Diff + Tangent Line
For the curve x² + xy + y² = 7: (a) find dy/dx implicitly, (b) find the equation of the tangent line at the point (1, 2).
Model Solution
(a) Differentiate x²+xy+y²=7 w.r.t. x:
2x + y + x(dy/dx) + 2y(dy/dx) = 0
(dy/dx)(x+2y) = −2x−y
dy/dx = −(2x+y)/(x+2y).

(b) Verify: 1+2+4=7 ✓. At (1,2): dy/dx = −(2+2)/(1+4) = −4/5.
Tangent: y−2 = −(4/5)(x−1) ⟹ y = −(4/5)x + 4/5 + 2 = −(4/5)x + 14/5.
Question 7 — Related Rates
Sand falls from a conveyor onto a conical pile at 3 m³/min. The cone always maintains height = diameter (h = 2r). How fast is the height increasing when h = 4 m?
Model Solution
Setup: V = (1/3)πr²h. Since h=2r: r=h/2. Substitute:
V = (1/3)π(h/2)²·h = (1/3)π·(h²/4)·h = πh³/12.

Differentiate w.r.t. t: dV/dt = (π/12)·3h²·(dh/dt) = (πh²/4)·(dh/dt).

Solve: At h=4, dV/dt=3:
3 = π(16)/4 · (dh/dt) = 4π · (dh/dt)
dh/dt = 3/(4π) m/min.
